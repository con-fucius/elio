## Digital Field-Work Execution & Control Platform — Kenya
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Requirements baseline for hostile review  
**Market:** Kenya; architecture intended to remain Africa-compatible  
**Scope:** One platform core, three domain profiles: healthcare field work, marketing/sales field work, debt collection/recovery

## 1. Product definition

The product is a field-work execution and control platform. It converts operational work into controlled, auditable field execution:

**Work Item → Assignment → Authority/Policy → Context → Execution → Evidence → Outcome → Review → Next Action**

The “work card” is the worker-facing presentation of a work item. It is not the core domain object.

The platform is not an EMR/EHR, CRM, loan-management system, accounting package, payment processor, route-optimisation product, HR system, or employee-surveillance product.

## 2. Problem

Kenyan field operations commonly depend on fragmented combinations of paper forms, spreadsheets, messaging, calls, generic CRMs, existing SFA tools and disconnected back-office systems. The operational problem is not merely digitising a form. It is maintaining trustworthy control over what was assigned, what the worker was authorised to do, what actually happened, what evidence exists, what outcome resulted, and what happens next.

The product must therefore optimise for operational integrity rather than form completion.

## 3. Target domains

### Healthcare field work
Examples: community/outreach visits, facility support, equipment/service interventions, inspections and other non-clinical or bounded field activities.

Boundary: do not replace the authoritative patient record or make clinical decisions in the core product.

Kenya’s Digital Health Act and health-information rules materially raise the compliance and interoperability burden for health data. Health deployments must be treated as regulated integrations, not ordinary SaaS tenants.

### Marketing/sales field work
Examples: outlet visits, merchandising, order capture, lead follow-up, promotion execution and market observations.

This is the lowest regulatory-risk domain but also the most commercially crowded. The product must not compete merely on GPS, offline forms, order capture or dashboards.

### Debt collection/recovery
Examples: authorised customer contact, visit, repayment arrangement, payment acknowledgement, dispute/escalation and follow-up.

The platform must encode conduct restrictions and authority boundaries. It must not facilitate harassment, shaming, unauthorised contact, threats, or disclosure of debt information.

## 4. Users

- Field worker
- Supervisor
- Operations manager
- Organisation administrator
- Compliance/audit user
- Platform administrator
- Integration/service account

The field worker is the primary design constraint: intermittent connectivity, limited attention, variable device quality, changing assignments and real-world exceptions are assumed.

## 5. Goals

1. Make authorised field work executable with minimal friction.
2. Preserve a trustworthy history of material actions.
3. Continue safe operation during connectivity and service degradation.
4. Make exceptions visible and recoverable.
5. Prevent unauthorised actions through policy, not training alone.
6. Integrate with existing systems instead of replacing them unnecessarily.
7. Provide management with operational truth rather than activity theatre.
8. Support three domains using one core without forcing identical workflows.
9. Make failure modes explicit, testable and observable.

## 6. Non-goals

- General-purpose ERP/CRM.
- Clinical decision support.
- Patient-record replacement.
- Loan origination/servicing.
- Payment processing or custody of funds.
- Employee surveillance.
- General-purpose workflow automation marketplace.
- AI agents taking consequential field actions.
- Hardware manufacturing.
- National-scale government platform in the initial product.

## 7. Product principles

- Correctness before feature count.
- Explicit state over implicit assumptions.
- Least privilege.
- Offline-first execution.
- No silent data loss.
- No silent policy bypass.
- Evidence has provenance and uncertainty.
- Human overrides are controlled and auditable.
- Domain-specific rules stay domain-specific.
- Integrations fail independently from core execution.
- Data minimisation by design.
- Operational metrics over vanity metrics.

## 8. Core workflow

A work item contains:
- unique immutable ID
- domain/type
- business context reference
- current state
- priority
- validity/due window
- required actions
- authority/policy reference
- evidence requirements
- outcome schema
- assignment history
- audit history

Indicative state model:

`draft → ready → assigned → accepted → in_progress → {completed, partially_completed, blocked, failed, cancelled} → review → closed`

Not every domain must expose every state. State transitions must be explicitly defined and validated.

## 9. Functional requirements

### FR-1 Work creation
Authorised users/integrations can create a work item with required context, policy and outcome schema.

### FR-2 Assignment
Work can be assigned, accepted, declined where allowed, reassigned, suspended or cancelled. Every material change is attributable.

### FR-3 Authority
The system evaluates whether an actor is allowed to perform an action under the applicable organisation, role, domain and work-item policy.

### FR-4 Context
Only necessary context is exposed to the worker. Sensitive fields require explicit policy and purpose.

### FR-5 Execution
Workers can execute structured steps, enter observations, record outcomes, attach evidence and report exceptions.

### FR-6 Offline
Previously synchronised authorised work remains executable offline. Local events are durable and queued for synchronisation.

### FR-7 Evidence
Evidence is stored with provenance, source type, timestamps and relationship to the action it supports.

### FR-8 Outcome
Completion must produce a typed outcome, not just a free-text note.

### FR-9 Exception handling
Workers can safely report blocked/unsafe/wrong/ambiguous work without falsifying completion.

### FR-10 Review
Supervisors can review configured exceptions and risk-based samples, approve/reject/correct/reassign, and document reasons.

### FR-11 Audit
Material actions are reconstructable chronologically.

### FR-12 Notifications
Notifications are advisory and non-authoritative. Failure of notification must not corrupt work state.

### FR-13 Search/reporting
Operational users can query work by state, assignee, domain, location/context, age, outcome and exception.

### FR-14 Integration
External systems can create/update/consume work through versioned APIs/events with idempotency.

### FR-15 Data rights
The platform supports workflows for access, correction, objection/deletion where legally applicable, retention and controlled export.

## 10. Domain-specific requirements

### Healthcare
- No clinical decision-making.
- Health data access is purpose/role constrained.
- Sensitive fields are minimised.
- Health-system integration must use approved interfaces/standards where applicable.
- Patient-facing or clinical functions require separate regulatory review.
- Audit trails must support health-information accountability.

### Marketing
- Visit objective is explicit.
- Commercial actions are typed.
- GPS/check-in is not treated as proof of performance.
- Customer/order systems remain authoritative where they already exist.
- Existing SFA capabilities should be integrated rather than duplicated.

### Collections
- Each collection action has an explicit authority/policy.
- Contact methods and escalation rules are policy-controlled.
- Restricted behaviour must be technically blocked where feasible.
- Payment acknowledgement is distinct from payment settlement.
- Disputes/complaints are first-class outcomes.
- No access to a customer's contact list for collection purposes.
- No public disclosure/shaming workflows.
- Collection communications are attributable and reviewable.

## 11. Non-functional requirements

Initial targets:
- No acknowledged material event silently disappears.
- Material actions are idempotent.
- Offline execution survives app restart and device reboot.
- Core execution remains available when notifications/analytics/integrations fail.
- All production changes are traceable.
- Backups are restorable, not merely configured.
- Security events are observable.
- P95 API latency target: ≤500 ms for ordinary online transactional operations under defined pilot load.
- Sync must be resumable and idempotent.
- Initial availability objective: ≥99.9% monthly for core backend excluding scheduled maintenance, subject to infrastructure validation.

## 12. Success criteria

A pilot succeeds only if:
- workers actually use it for real work;
- supervisors trust the resulting record;
- offline execution is reliable;
- exceptions are handled without workarounds;
- reconciliation is correct;
- measured administrative effort decreases;
- material audit reconstruction is possible;
- no critical privacy/security/control failure occurs.

A technically working application with poor worker adoption is a failed product.

## 13. Release boundary

Release 1 should contain:
- identity/access
- work creation
- assignment
- policy/authority
- mobile execution
- offline queue/sync
- evidence
- outcome
- exceptions
- supervisor review
- audit
- minimal reporting
- API/integration foundation

Explicitly defer:
- advanced route optimisation
- AI
- predictive analytics
- complex billing
- marketplace functionality
- large-scale workflow designer
- broad integrations before one real integration is validated
"""

## Version 0.1

## 1. Reliability

### NFR-R1 — Durable material events
Once the client confirms a material action as saved, it must survive app restart, process termination and ordinary device failure until synchronised or explicitly recovered.

### NFR-R2 — No silent loss
The system must expose unsynchronised, rejected, conflicted and failed events.

Every synchronisable command has a client-generated idempotency key. Replaying the same command produces one logical effect.

### NFR-R4 — Recovery
The mobile client must recover its local operation log after:
- crash
- forced close
- OS restart
- temporary storage pressure where recoverable
- network disappearance

## 2. Offline

The app must support a defined offline working window for the pilot, target 8–12 hours of ordinary work.

Offline capabilities:
- assigned work
- authorised context
- workflow execution
- structured input
- evidence capture
- exception creation
- local audit chain

Not necessarily available offline:
- live reassignment
- live policy changes
- external verification
- server-only analytics

The UI must clearly show:
- last successful sync
- pending events
- rejected/conflicted events
- policy/context staleness

## 3. Synchronisation

Use an append-oriented client event model rather than treating the client as a second mutable server database.

Every event includes:
- event ID
- work-item ID
- aggregate/entity ID
- actor/device/session
- local event timestamp
- sequence/version where applicable
- payload
- schema version
- policy version
- previous local event hash if tamper-evident chaining is enabled

Server processing:
1. authenticate
2. validate schema
3. validate authority
4. validate current state
5. apply deterministic conflict policy
6. persist accepted/rejected result
7. return authoritative state

Never use blind last-write-wins for material operational events.

Pilot targets:
- P95 normal API response ≤500 ms.
- P99 ≤2 s for ordinary transactional requests.

- Abuse detection.
- Centralised secrets management.

docs["R5_FAILURE_RESILIENCE.md"] = r"""# R5 — Failure, Drift & Resilience Model
## Version 0.1

## 1. Principle

The system must assume failure, not merely detect it.

A failure is acceptable only when:
- its impact is bounded,
- the system remains understandable,
- recovery is possible,
- material state is not silently corrupted,
- the event is observable.

## 2. Failure classes

### Connectivity
- no network
- intermittent network
- captive portal
- high latency
- server unavailable

Expected behaviour: local execution within authorised offline envelope; durable queue; visible sync state.

### Device
- battery depletion
- crash
- storage exhaustion
- OS kill
- lost device
- damaged camera
- wrong device time

Expected behaviour: durable local state; reauthentication/recovery; no reliance on volatile memory.

### Human
- wrong work item
- wrong customer
- accidental completion
- duplicate tap
- misunderstanding
- fatigue
- malicious action

Expected behaviour: confirmation where consequential; undo/recovery where safe; explicit exceptions; audit.

### Organisation
- worker leaves
- supervisor unavailable
- reassignment
- policy change
- sudden workload spike
- organisational restructure

Expected behaviour: assignments can change without corrupting history; authority is evaluated explicitly.

### Integration
- timeout
- duplicate callback
- schema change
- external outage
- partial success

Expected behaviour: core work state remains independent; integration has reconciliation state.

### Data
- malformed input
- conflicting edits
- stale context
- corruption

Expected behaviour: validation, conflict state, recovery process; never silent overwrite.

## 3. Offline authority envelope

Not all actions are equally safe offline.

Classify actions:

**Class A — safe offline**
- record observation
- draft outcome
- capture evidence
- create exception

**Class B — conditionally offline**
- complete work
- customer acknowledgement
- create follow-up

**Class C — online required**
- change financial obligation
- final payment settlement
- revoke/alter high-risk authority
- access newly restricted sensitive data
- high-consequence health action

The platform must encode these classifications.

## 4. Conflict handling

Conflicts must be semantic.

Examples:
- two supervisors reassign same work
- worker completes work after cancellation
- two devices edit same observation
- external system closes a case while field worker is offline

Resolution options:
- automatic merge for independent fields
- server precedence for authoritative state
- explicit conflict queue
- supervisor review

Never silently discard one material event.

## 5. Drift detection

Drift indicators:
- increasing completion without evidence
- abnormal offline durations
- rising correction rate
- unusual override frequency
- repeated policy denials
- sync backlog
- increased duplicate events
- unusual worker travel patterns
- rising supervisor bulk approvals

Drift is a signal for investigation, not automatic accusation.

## 6. Near-miss model

Record:
- event
- expected state
- observed state
- detection point
- impact
- containment
- root/system cause
- corrective action
- whether similar events exist

Near misses must feed engineering and operations reviews.

## 7. Control loop

Operational control:

**Observe → Compare against policy/expected state → Decide → Act → Verify → Learn**

The platform should support this loop but must not pretend that every decision can be automated.

## 8. Economic stress

Assume:
- low-cost devices
- staff turnover
- expensive mobile data
- power interruptions
- high workload
- limited training
- pressure to meet targets

Design responses:
- low data transfer
- local queueing
- compressed evidence
- minimal typing
- progressive sync
- clear recovery
- training that focuses on exceptions, not manuals

## 9. Disaster scenarios

At minimum test:
- 24-hour backend outage
- 12-hour field offline period
- mass reassignment
- lost supervisor access
- compromised worker account
- database restore
- object-storage outage
- external API outage
- mobile app upgrade during unsynchronised work
- policy update while workers are offline

## 10. Resilience acceptance criteria

A pilot is not resilient until:
- no material work disappears under tested failure scenarios;
- recovery is understandable to a field worker;
- supervisors can reconcile exceptions;
- audit history remains coherent;
- external outages do not corrupt internal state.
"""

## 2. Relationships

WorkItem 1→N Exceptions

## 3. Invariants

- IDs are globally unique.
- Tenant ID is mandatory on tenant-owned objects.
- Work item state transitions are validated server-side.
- An action cannot occur without an authorised actor.
- A closed work item cannot be mutated directly.
- Corrections create new events; history is not rewritten.
- Evidence cannot silently move between work items.
- External references are not treated as authoritative without source designation.
- Payment acknowledgement is not settlement.
- Health context is not exposed merely because a user has a generic field role.

## 4. Event model

Prefer immutable domain events for material changes:

`WorkAssigned`
`WorkAccepted`
`WorkStarted`
`ActionRecorded`
`EvidenceCaptured`
`OutcomeRecorded`
`ExceptionRaised`
`WorkCompleted`
`ReviewSubmitted`
`WorkReopened`
`WorkClosed`

Commands request state change; events record what actually happened.

## 5. Event ordering

Each device maintains local sequence numbers. The server assigns authoritative receipt/order metadata.

Do not assume device clocks are trustworthy.

Use:
- client event time for contextual timing
- server receipt time for system chronology
- monotonic/local sequence for ordering local events

## 6. Data classification

Suggested classes:
- Public
- Internal
- Confidential
- Sensitive personal
- Highly sensitive/regulated

Healthcare data and certain financial/personal information require stricter policies.

## 7. Retention

Retention is defined per data class and purpose, not globally.

Evidence retention may differ from operational work retention.

Audit records may need longer retention than ordinary UI data.

Deletion must preserve legally required records and evidentiary integrity.

## 8. API resource model

Primary resources:
- organisations
- actors
- work-items
- assignments
- actions
- evidence
- outcomes
- exceptions
- reviews
- policies
- audit-events
- integrations

`POST /work-items/{id}/assign`
`POST /work-items/{id}/actions`
`POST /work-items/{id}/complete`
`POST /work-items/{id}/exceptions`

Avoid generic “update everything” endpoints for material state.

## 9. Integration model

External systems interact through:
- authenticated REST APIs
- signed webhooks where appropriate
- asynchronous events
- scheduled reconciliation

Every integration has:
- source of truth definition
- contract version
- retry policy
- idempotency strategy
- reconciliation process
- failure state

## 10. Tenant isolation

Tenant identity must be enforced:
- at authentication
- service layer
- database query layer
- object storage
- background jobs
- analytics/reporting

Automated cross-tenant tests are mandatory.
